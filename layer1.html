Layer 1 Blockchains

A Layer 1 (L1) is a base blockchain with its own validators and consensus rules. It processes and finalizes transactions itself, and it is what other layers ultimately rely on. Bitcoin, Ethereum, and Solana are examples. Of these, Ethereum and Solana run general smart contracts.

What a Layer 1 Does

  • Orders transactions and agrees on one shared history (consensus)
  • Executes smart contract code
  • Pays validators with fees and rewards, which secures the network

The Scalability Tradeoff

Blockchains are often described through three goals: security, decentralization, and scalability. This is sometimes called the blockchain trilemma. Making blocks larger or faster raises throughput, but it also raises the hardware needed to run a node, which can push the network toward fewer, larger operators. Different Layer 1 chains choose different balances.

Why Fees Rise

Block space is limited. When many people want to use a chain at once, they bid against each other with higher gas fees. This pressure is the main reason Layer 2 networks exist. Continue to Layer 2 Solutions.
